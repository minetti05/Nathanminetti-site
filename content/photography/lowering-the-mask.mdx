This series traces a very particular moment — the first tentative return to being near other people after months of not being near anyone. It happens in parks, at outdoor tables with a chair pulled six feet from the next one, in the space between strangers relearning how to occupy a sidewalk together. Two friends sharing a park bench in the last light of evening. Someone lighting a stranger's cigarette. A couple leaning toward each other across a candlelit café table, close enough to talk, careful enough to keep their distance. A birthday cake balanced on someone's knee in the dark, pizza boxes fanned open across a blanket in the grass — small, deliberate gatherings, staged around whatever the moment allowed.

The mask was literal that year, and also something else. Lowering it — even briefly, even outdoors, even six feet apart — meant deciding to be seen again, to let a stranger or a friend close enough to matter. There was a newfound innocence to it: meeting someone's eyes across an outdoor table felt different that season, less automatic, more like something you'd chosen. Nobody in these photographs is posing for long; I was more interested in the specific, unrepeatable moment of people finding their way back toward each other, tentatively, outdoors, letting their guard down before they were entirely sure it was safe to.

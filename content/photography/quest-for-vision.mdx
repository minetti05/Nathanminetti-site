This series began as a road trip — San Francisco east through the Mojave, into Sedona's red rock, north through Santa Fe, Colorado, and Utah, back down the coast through San Diego — and became something closer to a search. Not for landmarks, but for the moments that made me look twice: snow sitting improbably on desert sand, like the landscape had contradicted itself. Two lone tents pitched on an empty beach, the only sign of anyone for miles. A water tower rising over a small Southwestern town, plain and strange against the sky. A cliff's edge reached only after a long hike, chasing a specific angle that existed nowhere else.

This wasn't a trip to check landmarks off a list. It was a quest for a kind of seeing — for the unfamiliar detail hiding inside a familiar landscape, for the shot that required getting somewhere on foot rather than pulling over. What I came back with is less a travelogue than a record of paying attention: proof that the West still had things in it I didn't already know how to see.

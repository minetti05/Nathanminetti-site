This series began as an experiment — a  roll of black-and-white film loaded into a camera that usually shot in color — and became its own way of seeing the city. Stripped of color, landmarks stop being postmarks and start being forms: the Brooklyn Bridge collapses into a single vanishing point down the center of its walkway; a stone eagle's wing becomes pure sculptural weight against bare branches; a cannon tucked under a park arcade reads less as history and more as shape and shadow.

Without color to soften or narrate the frame, what's left is structure, light, and the particular geometry of a city built in stone, iron, and bronze. A tree's shadow falling across untouched snow, the outline of my own body cast into that same shadow — these images are less about documenting a place than about noticing it, closely and slowly, at a moment when there was little else to do but look.

Where the color work is a record of a specific season, this series is closer to a study: an attempt to find the bones underneath a city I thought I already knew.
